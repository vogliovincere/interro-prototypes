Settlement Account Details
Research & Design Decisions

What four real private-credit administrative details forms actually contain, what the market convention turned out to be, and why the prototype is shaped the way it is — including the places where we chose to go beyond the evidence, and said so.

← Open the prototype

Adam Herring raised Admin Details forms as the point in a private-credit relationship where wire details are collected. This is the research behind a third prototype built for that moment.

The existing two widgets are both anchored to a payment: the capital-call flow displays instructions for money coming in, the distribution flow collects one account for a distribution already declared. Private credit onboarding is not anchored to a payment at all. A lender publishes its settlement details once, as a standing record, before any particular payment exists — and that record routinely holds several accounts. Nothing in either existing widget can represent that, which is why this is a third prototype rather than a variant of the second.

Section 2The five findings that shaped the design

2.1  DDA and FFC are not two account types. They are two levels of one address.

This is the finding the whole prototype is built on, and the easy mistake is to treat DDA and FFC as a pair of sibling tags you pick between. They are not. DDA is the Demand Deposit Account the routing number actually reaches; FFC — "for further credit" — is the sub-account inside it that the money must then be applied on to. One address, two levels, and both are needed: 

Name of Bank          <trustee> Agency & Trust - New York
ABA Number            <routing number>         ← level 1 is what this reaches
DDA Account Number    <trustee's account>      ← LEVEL 1  (Demand Deposit Account)
DDA Account Name      <the CLO's name>
FFC Account Name      <the CLO's name> / Collection Account
FFC Account Number    <sub-account>            ← LEVEL 2  (for further credit)
Why this matters more than it looks

Get level 1 right and level 2 wrong and the wire arrives — and is never credited to the party. It sits unapplied in the custodian's account. That is materially worse than a rejection, because a rejection produces an error somebody chases and this produces silence.

The two older forms (the 2005 Questionnaire and the completed CLO form) both carry an explicit FFC Account Name / FFC Account No. pair. The 2018 ADF has no equivalent field at all — it expects the sub-account either to be the beneficiary account (its Template 1, "lender has dedicated account") or to be resolved from the reference line. That is the single largest modelling divergence between the four documents and a real data-loss risk for anyone mapping the old forms onto the new one.

2.2  The purpose tag already exists — as free text buried in a name.

No form has a purpose or account-type field. The completed CLO form encodes it in the account name:

FFC Account Name:  <entity> / Collection Account

Account Name:  <entity> / Custody Account

Two sub-accounts, at the same custodian, inside the same DDA, distinguished by a phrase a human typed into a name field. That value is unqueryable, unvalidatable, and invisible to any system downstream. Promoting it to a first-class tag is the clearest single thing this widget adds, and it costs the user one tap.

2.3  Currency is the axis of multiplicity. Payment purpose is not.

This was the finding most likely to have been got wrong by intuition, so it was checked hardest. Splitting accounts by principal / interest / fees feels natural and is not the convention.

Currency, in contrast, is unambiguously the key. The ADF's Appendix A says so:

"Lender may copy and paste the template below as many times as necessary to capture various currency remittance instructions."

Currency is the first field of every instruction block; ADFlow's pitch is to "self manage all currency SSIs"; ISDA's Standing Settlement Instructions SOP makes currency a mandatory component of an SSI; SWIFT's MT 670/671 SSI-update messages carry a mandatory, repeating sequence keyed on currency. Agents publish their own remittance instructions the same way — separate USD and CAD blocks, one reference each.

Purpose, meanwhile, appears in exactly one place across every edition of the ADF:

Payment Reference     Use Standard Wire Reference Format*:
(Remittance Info)     [Borrower Name]
                      [Facility Name/Abbr.] [Facility/Deal CUSIP/ISIN]
                      [Payment Purpose(s)] [Transaction Reference ID]

Inside the reference line, beside four other unambiguously narrative tokens. There is no purpose field at the top of the block and no repeat-per-purpose instruction anywhere — in pointed contrast to the explicit repeat-per-currency instruction. The completed CLO form makes the same choice by hand: one account receives both, and its reference reads Loan Name & [Principal or Interest] — a bracketed either/or still sitting unresolved in the template.

A search of SEC EDGAR full text for lender instructions splitting principal from interest returned nothing usable. The closest hits actively disprove it: one purchaser schedule is labelled "Wire Instructions for Principal/Interest/" followed by a single account — the label enumerates purposes precisely because one account takes all of them.

Where per-purpose accounts genuinely are real

Adjacent markets, and worth not confusing with this one. Mortgage servicing requires escrow (T&I) funds segregated from P&I funds. CLO indentures maintain distinct Interest Collection and Principal Collection accounts at the trustee. Both are payer-side legal-segregation or waterfall constructs. On the payee side in syndicated lending, a lender receives everything into one account per currency and reconciles from reference data.

2.4  The market is deliberately removing phone callbacks.

This determines how the flow can end. S&P Global's ADFlow brochure is explicit — institutions have

"been able to move away from laborious and outdated call-back requirements for SSIs and reduce the risk of misdirected payments"

with "Remove Call-Backs" named as a headline benefit. What replaces it is a maker/checker approval — "a Maker/Checker process is a requirement for data review and publication" — plus version history, a diff tool, and permissioned distribution. The 2018 ADF's only nod to verification is a contact tagged SSI Verification; the 2005 Questionnaire has no verification concept whatsoever, which is the gap the newer machinery was built to close.

2.5  Three of the four documents are mostly not about wires.

The 2018 ADF surrounds its one wire block with MEI, LEI, GIIN, CRN, EIN, country of incorporation, country of tax residence, UK treaty passport number and expiry, a signature-block drafting field, and five separate categories of contact. The 2005 Questionnaire devotes its entire final page to a W-8BEN / W-8ECI / W-8EXP / W-8IMY / W-9 decision tree, including the instruction that faxed forms are not acceptable and an original must be submitted.

None of that affects where a wire goes. A settlement widget should consume a resolved entity, not re-collect its identifiers — which is why this prototype collects account details only, across four screens, and asks nothing about the party at all.

For the record, since it is the only place any of the four forms prints its enum: the 2005 Questionnaire's lender-type list reads Bank, Asset Manager, Broker/Dealer, CLO/CDO, Finance Company, Hedge Fund, Insurance, Mutual Fund, Pension Fund, Other Regulated Investment Fund, Special Purpose Vehicle, Other — please specify. Useful for an entity-master form; not this one.

Section 3Design decisions

DecisionWhyBasis
A separate prototype, not a variant of the distribution flow Onboarding is not initiated by a payment and produces a list of accounts, not one. The distribution widget's entire shape — one country, one field set, one submit — is wrong for it. Evidenced
Two-level accounts, asked as a plain-language question The ADF ships two templates and tells the reader which to use: Template 1 "where … lender has dedicated account", Appendix A "where recipient is intermediary bank with nostro account for custodian and the Lender does not have a dedicated account". That is a real either/or with different field sets. Printing both and hoping the reader picks correctly is how it goes wrong, so the widget asks instead. Evidenced
A first-class purpose tag on every account Replaces a convention currently carried as free text inside an account name. Colour-coded on the card, because the point of promoting it is that it becomes scannable. Evidenced
Bank country asked per account, not per party Falls out of currency being the axis. A fund with a USD collection account in New York and a EUR account in Frankfurt is ordinary, and one party-level country question could not express it. This also lets the existing 40-jurisdiction field engine drive each account independently. Evidenced
No checking-or-savings question The US field set inherited from the distribution widget asks it as a required select. A wire does not need the answer: Fedwire routes on the ABA and applies on the account number, and the message carries no deposit-type field. The question is real on retail ACH forms, where the type is part of the instruction, and it stays in the shared schema because the distribution widget collects from individuals. Here the counterparties are trustees, custodians and CLOs, so the answer is "checking" every time — a required field with one possible answer is friction that buys nothing and implies the wire could be misrouted without it. Design judgment
Purpose stays out of the account key Follows the convention rather than inventing one: no principal-versus-interest split, because that distinction belongs in the reference line (§2.3). An earlier build also showed the assembled reference line on the account screen, and that was cut after review — describing what a future payment will carry, on a screen that moves no money, read as though something were being processed. The finding stands; the widget is simply not the place to display it. If it reappears it belongs next to a payment. Evidenced
Wide embedded frame by default, mobile layout retained below 900px The other two prototypes are 390px because they were scoped as mobile widgets. This one is destined for websites, so it defaults to a 980px embedded frame: short fixed-length fields pair two to a row, free-text names and addresses keep full width, choices and saved accounts go two-up, and the reading column stays capped so prose never runs to a 900px measure. It is a wider frame rather than a redesign — same components, same brand. Every wide rule is gated behind a media query, so the single-column mobile flow is intact underneath rather than replaced. Design judgment
The browser back button works Each screen change pushes a history entry, so back and forward walk the flow instead of leaving the prototype — which in an embedded context would navigate the host page away. Going back does not unwind saved accounts: back moves between screens, it is not an undo stack. A side benefit is that the URL always reflects the current screen, so any point in the flow can be copied out of the address bar and shared. Design judgment
"Add another account?" as a second step on the same page The answer is usually no. A full screen transition to say no is a worse experience than a question appearing where the work just finished. Step 1 keeps a running list of what is already added, so the context never disappears. Design judgment
A second account inherits the first one's bank The common case is the same custodian with a different purpose. The tag and the sub-account are what differ, so those alone start blank and the next unused tag is pre-selected. Design judgment
Ends at maker/checker, not at a callback Matches where the market is going. The callback ending is built too, in amber rather than green, so the two can be compared — which model Interro adopts is a policy decision this prototype should surface, not settle. Evidenced
No entity fields at all An earlier draft opened with a screen collecting legal name, party type, manager, registered address, country and an operations contact. It is gone. This is a component that does one thing: collect the coordinates money can be sent to. Everything about who the party is belongs to the entity master and the KYC flow, and a widget that re-collects it is both a worse widget and a worse integration. §2.5 is the evidence: the real forms bury one wire block inside pages of identity and tax material, none of which affects where a wire goes.

Consequence worth noting: the ADF's SSI Verification contact went with it. If Interro wants that captured here rather than inherited from the party record, it is one optional field on review.		 Evidenced
Account numbers masked to the last four on read-back A summary screen is a screenshot. Routing numbers stay visible — they are public — and account and FFC numbers do not. Design judgment
Hyphens stripped before validating The completed CLO form writes its DDA number with a dash in it. Someone transcribing from a PDF will type the dash; rejecting it would be the form being wrong, not the user. Evidenced

What was deliberately not built

  • Non-cash settlement rails. The completed CLO form carries book-entry/DTC, free physical delivery and DVP delivery instructions alongside its cash wire. That is securities delivery — a different domain, and absorbing it drags in DTC participant codes and physical mail addresses.
  • Payment method (MT vs ISO 20022). The March 2023 ADF adds a Payment Method field — MT103 or MT202 — and an alternate ISO 20022 block keyed PACS.008 or PACS.009, with Creditor / Creditor Agent replacing Beneficiary / Account With Institution. Real, current, and a genuine gap in this prototype. Left out to keep the account form to one screen; it is the most defensible thing to add next.
  • The 2005 form's agent-side block. Where the lender sends money to is deal setup, not the lender onboarding its own details.
  • Data-room and document-distribution permissioning (Data Room Access, Doc. Delivery, Recon & Inventory) and the four non-payment contact roles (credit, letter of credit, bid, private/public side). Entitlements plumbing. The only contacts a settlement widget needs are operations and SSI verification.

Section 4Where we went beyond the evidence

Two of the four purpose tags are ours, not the market's, and the widget should not pretend otherwise.

TagBasisStanding
Collection account Verbatim from the completed CLO form's own FFC account name, and the account behind its "WIRE INSTRUCTIONS FOR LOAN PRINCIPAL AND INTEREST PAYMENTS" section. Verified
Custody account Verbatim, same source — a separately numbered sub-account at the same custodian. Corroborated by ISDA's SSI asset categories, which distinguish SECU (securities) from COPA (commercial payments). Verified
Funding account Not a tag on any of the four forms. The 2005 Questionnaire implies the direction split by capturing the agent's instructions separately; we have made it a tag instead. Inferred
Other Escape hatch with a free-text label, matching the ADF's own "If lender/entity type does not appear in list, you may provide your own value." Inferred

One framing correction worth keeping straight, because the widget's own wording could mislead: the tag asks "what is this account for?", but it is not a payment-purpose field. Payment purpose (principal vs interest) stays in the reference line, per §2.3. What the tag actually captures is closer to asset class and direction — cash in, securities, cash out — which is the axis ISDA and DTCC key their SSIs on, and is therefore defensible. It is the label that is loose, not the model.

Section 5Not verified, and open questions

Read this before quoting anything above

The second research track — private-credit settlement operations, CLO trustee mechanics, and the fraud-control literature — did not complete. It stopped partway through on an account spend limit. What is below is what remains genuinely unanswered, not a tidy list of nice-to-haves.

  • CLO trustee mechanics. Whether a wire to a CLO should name the trustee rather than the issuer as beneficiary, and how the indenture's collection/payment accounts relate to the FFC sub-accounts a counterparty sees, is unconfirmed. The completed form is consistent with the trustee holding level 1, but one instance is not a rule.
  • BDCs, insurance separate accounts, SMAs and funds-of-one — not researched. Any of them could add a structure the two-level model does not hold.
  • Whether a maker/checker flow is actually sufficient under whatever policy Interro operates, or whether a callback is required for a change even if not for initial setup. The literature consistently treats changes as higher-risk than first-time setup; the prototype currently treats them alike.
  • The current (28 January 2025) ADF edition. Member-gated. Field labels here are from the 2018 and 2023 editions.
  • Per-purpose accounts as a bespoke arrangement. Roughly a one-in-ten residual: a fund privately telling an agent "fees to the management company, principal and interest to the custodian" would never appear in a public filing. If we support it, it should be an optional override, never the primary axis.
  • Vocabulary check with an actual private-credit ops professional. The prototype's copy has not been read by one. Terms that are safe for both private credit and private equity — the brief asked for both — were chosen on judgment, not tested.

The prototype contains fabricated demo data. The worked CLO example reproduces the structure of a real completed admin-details form supplied privately by Quorim; every name and number in it is invented, and the source document is not in this repository — publishing a live entity's real wire coordinates is the raw material for exactly the misdirected-payment fraud this market's verification apparatus exists to prevent.

Jurisdiction field requirements come from International Wire Address Formats v2; see that report for sourcing and for the requirements flagged there as unverified.
